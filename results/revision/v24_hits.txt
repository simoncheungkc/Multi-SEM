Spatial dependence in empirical systems often operates simultaneously across continuous physical distance, administrative jurisdictions, and functional networks. Conventional spatial error models condition on a single spatial weight matrix, potentially overlooking overlapping relational channels and distorting inference. This paper develops Multiplex Spatial Error Regression (Multi-SEM), an integrated econometric pipeline combining ex-ante structural pruning, topology-calibrated residual screening, stability-constrained Bayesian estimation, and precision-form spatial kriging. An additive disturbance specification preserves linear conditional-mean interpretability ({E}{Y}{X}{=Xβ}) under exogeneity, while the parameter restriction {‖λ}{‖}{1}{=}{s=1}{k}{|}{λ}{s}{|<1} establishes a uniform sufficient condition for spectral stability across row-substochastic layers. We derive exact design-conditional null moments for residual Moran's {I} under isolate scaling and introduce topology-triggered Monte Carlo calibration to correct finite-sample size distortions on hub-dominated networks prior to false discovery rate control. For out-of-sample prediction, formulating spatial kriging through the full-graph precision matrix ({Q=}{σ}{-2}{V}{λ}{'}{V}{λ}) reduces the computational complexity of the holdout solve from {O}{n}{t}{3} to {O}{n}{*}{3}. In an empirical application to {n=506} census tracts across 92 municipalities in Greater Boston, covariance-adapted Multi-SEM jointly represents candidate spatial dependence associated with continuous geographic proximity, a multi-hop polynomial spatial filter, and discrete municipal jurisdictions. Across 50 repeated random 80/20 splits, Multi-SEM reduces mean holdout RMSE by 28.5% relative to OLS ({0.1375} vs. {0.1921}; Nadeau–Bengio resampled {t}{49}{=7.29}, {p=2.34×}{10}{-9}, outperforming OLS on 50 of 50 splits) and achieves a 2.0% incremental error reduction over a single-layer geographic SEM ({0.1403}), attaining lower error on 37 of 50 splits (conservative resampled {t}{49}{=1.11}, {p=0.272}). Across 63 monthly S&P100 equity cross-sections, Multi-SEM achieved lower holdout RMSE than OLS in 44 months (sign test {p=0.0022}), although autocorrelation-robust inference for the monthly loss differential was less decisive (Newey–West {p=0.072}).

Tobler's first law characterizes spatial dependence through continuous distance decay, whereby nearby observations tend to be more strongly related than distant ones (Tobler, 1970). Yet administrative, legal, and functional boundaries often intersect with this geographic gradient. A residential property in Greater Boston has contiguous geographic neighbours, but also belongs to a municipal jurisdiction that determines property tax assessment, zoning bylaws, and public school provision (Harrison and Rubinfeld, 1978; Gilley and Pace, 1996). The same intersection of affiliation and co-movement arises among firms. Standard spatial regressions traditionally condition on a single weight matrix {W} (Bivand et al., 2014; Bivand and Wong, 2018). While higher-order spatial autoregressive and error models have been analyzed since Anselin (1988) and Lacombe (2004), empirical practice remains constrained by the challenge of testing, stabilizing, and estimating multiple non-commuting spatial matrices. Multi-SEM resolves this limitation through a unified pipeline combining structural pruning, calibrated screening, {l}{1}-constrained Bayesian estimation, and precision-domain spatial interpolation.

===== 26 =====
Higher-order spatial autoregressive and spatial-error specifications have been part of the classical toolkit since Anselin (1988). Lacombe (2004) showed that conclusions about a substantive spatial effect can change when the specification moves beyond a single weight matrix, and Lee and Liu (2010) gave efficient GMM theory for high-order spatial models with autoregressive disturbances. Recent empirical practice nevertheless remains dominated by the selection of one matrix {W}. Weight-matrix uncertainty has been addressed by Bayesian model averaging (LeSage and Fischer, 2008; Piribauer and Crespo Cuaresma, 2016) and by penalized selection (Lam and Souza, 2020). Multi-SEM does not claim a new mathematical model class. It provides one reproducible pipeline that combines ex-ante structural pruning, design-conditional screening, {l}{1}-constrained Bayesian estimation, and precision-domain spatial interpolation.
