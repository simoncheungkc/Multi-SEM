004 Abstract
005 Many empirical systems are inherently multiplex: interconnected entities interact simultaneously across physical space, administrative jurisdictions, and functional relational networks. This paper develops Multiplex Spatial Error Regression (Multi-SEM), an integrated econometric pipeline that combines ex-ante structural pruning, topology-calibrated residual screening, [l][1]-constrained Bayesian estimation, and precision-form spatial kriging.
006 Multi-SEM addresses four methodological challenges. First, an additive disturbance specification preserves the linear conditional-mean interpretation, [E][Y][X][=Xβ], under exogeneity while avoiding the endogeneity associated with spatially lagged dependent variables. Second, the parameter restriction [‖λ][‖][1][=][s=1][k][|][λ][s][|<1]provides a uniform sufficient condition for spectral stability across row-substochastic network layers. Third, candidate layers are screened using residual Moran's [I], with exact design-conditional null moments incorporating isolate scaling; for hub-dominated topologies, conditional Monte Carlo calibration is applied prior to false discovery rate control. Fourth, unobserved nodes are imputed through precision kriging, with [Q=][σ][-2][V][λ]['][V][λ], reducing computational complexity from [O][n][t][3] to [O][n][*][3].
007 We evaluate Multi-SEM using two substantive empirical benchmarks. In a spatial hedonic study of [n=506] Boston census tracts across 92 municipalities, Multi-SEM simultaneously captures geographic distance decay, regional multi-hop spillovers, and municipal boundaries. Across 50 repeated random holdout splits, the covariance-adapted Multi-SEM achieves an average holdout RMSE of 0.1375, a 28.5% reduction relative to OLS (0.1921; Wilcoxon [p=1.78×][10][-15]) and a 2.0% reduction relative to the single-layer geographic SEM (0.1403), and it outperforms OLS on 50 of 50 splits. A 63-month longitudinal panel of S&P 100 equity returns confirms that the pipeline scales to dynamic, non-geographic networks, outperforming OLS in 44 of 63 months (sign test [p=0.0022]; Newey–West [p=0.072]). Those equity fits used the isotropic proposal.
008 Keywords: Multiple spatial weight matrices; Spatial Error Model; Residual Moran's I; Precision kriging; Hedonic housing; S&P100.
011 1. Introduction
012 In interconnected economic and financial systems, cross-sectional observations are rarely independent. Latent shocks omitted environmental variables, and common industry factors can induce residual dependence across observational units. Under strictly exogenous regressors, [E][u][X][=0], ordinary least squares remains consistent but inefficient, may yield distorted standard errors, and fails to exploit informative residual dependence in out-of-sample prediction.
013 Tobler's first law characterizes spatial dependence through continuous distance decay, whereby nearby observations tend to be more strongly related than distant ones (Tobler, 1970). Yet administrative, legal, and functional boundaries often intersect with this geographic gradient. A residential property in Greater Boston has contiguous geographic neighbours, but also belongs to a municipal jurisdiction that determines property tax assessment, zoning bylaws, and public school provision (Harrison and Rubinfeld, 1978; Gilley and Pace, 1996). Similarly, a publicly traded firm belongs to an industry group while also being linked to corporate peers through return co-movement. Standard spatial regressions traditionally condition on a single weight matrix [W] (Bivand et al., 2014; Bivand and Wong, 2018). A single spatial operator cannot simultaneously accommodate these distinct relational channels. Multi-SEM resolves this limitation by testing, screening, and combining candidate weight matrices within a unified model while enforcing a uniform sufficient condition for invertibility of the spatial filter.
014 Bivand and Wong (2018) compare implementations of global and local indicators of spatial association, including Moran's [I], and show that the numerical results depend on the weights and on the software defaults used to construct them. Bivand, Gómez-Rubio, and Rue (2014) develop approximate Bayesian inference for spatial econometric models that each condition on one weight matrix, and they compare those models with a latent Gaussian Markov random field. Median housing prices in Boston census tracts are one of their applications.

Structurally redundant transformations, including clique resolvents with [r][vec][≥0.99], are removed prior to testing. Holdout outcomes are then imputed using the precision matrix

024 2. Related Work and Methodological Background
025 Spatial econometrics distinguishes dependence in observed outcomes (SAR; Anselin, 2009; LeSage & Pace, 2009) from dependence in disturbances (SEM; Kelejian & Prucha, 2010). A spatial lag [AY]renders OLS inconsistent, whereas a spatial error specification preserves [E][Y][X][=Xβ] under exogeneity.
026 With multiple spatial operators, a multiplicative filter such as [I-][λ][1][A][1][I-][λ][2][A][2][u=ε] generates cross-products such as [A][1][A][2], whose interpretation becomes difficult after row normalization. The additive specification
027 [u=][s=1][k][λ][s][A][s][u+ε]
028 avoids these interaction terms and provides a more direct representation of dependence across multiple layers (Florax & Nijkamp, 2005; Bivand et al., 2014).
029 Bivand, Gómez-Rubio, and Rue (2014) estimate such single-weight spatial econometric models using integrated nested Laplace approximation and compare them with correlated Gaussian random effects. The random-effect specification is a Gaussian Markov random field (Rue and Held, 2005). Their framework, however, considers a single spatial operator. Allowing several overlapping operators that do not share a common eigenspace introduces an additional challenge: for row-substochastic layers, restricting the coefficient vector to the open [l][1] ball, [‖λ‖][​][1][<1], provides a uniform sufficient condition ensuring invertibility of the resulting multiplex filter without requiring eigenvalue recomputation across proposals.
030 Three further issues are important in the multiplex setting. First, an auxiliary regression of [Y] on [A][s][Y] tests for an endogenous spatial lag rather than disturbance dependence (Manski, 1993; Lee, 2002); residual Moran's [I]is therefore more closely aligned with the SEM specification. Second, imposing only the signed-sum restriction [s=1][k][λ][s][<1]does not prevent large coefficients with opposite signs from making [V][λ] singular. Third, Katz resolvents of affiliation cliques lie in [span][⁡][J][I] and, once the diagonal is removed and the rows are standardized, reproduce the base clique rather than providing a distinct network layer.

049 If every layer is fully row-stochastic, then
050 [V][λ][1][n][=][1-][s=1][k][λ][s][1][n][.]
051 As the signed sum [s=1][k][λ][s] approaches one, the filtered intercept contracts toward zero, increasing the posterior variance of [β][0]. 057 3.4 Affiliation Clique Resolvents and Structural Redundancy
058 A Katz resolvent is defined as [G][A][=A][I-αA][-1][.] For a zero-diagonal clique

209 7.5 Induced subgraph versus the coherent marginal likelihood
210 We evaluate the finite-sample performance of the induced-subgraph estimator relative to the coherent marginal-likelihood estimator at two sample sizes, [n=200] and [n=500], using a [20%] node-holdout design [n][*][n][=0.20]. At [n=500], we conduct 20 replications using concentrated profile likelihood. Under both approaches, out-of-sample prediction is performed by precision-form kriging based on the full-graph precision matrix [Q]. The training criterion is evaluated using either the induced, re-row-standardized subgraph [A][T,T] or the coherent Schur marginal precision [P][T][=][Q][tt][-][Q][t*][Q][**][-1][Q][*t][.]
211 At [n=200], where the true autoregressive parameters are [λ][1][λ][2][=][0.40][0.35], the mean concentrated-likelihood estimates are [0.305, 0.265]for the induced-subgraph estimator, corresponding to biases of [-0.095,-0.085], and [0.339][0.313] for the marginal-likelihood estimator, with biases of [-0.061,-0.037]. Posterior means from the Gibbs sampler exhibit a similar difference, with estimates of [0.321][0.268] under the induced-subgraph approach and [0.367][0.314] under the marginal likelihood. The regression coefficients are also stable, with median relative differences of 3.7% for the slope coefficients and 4.6% for the full vector [β]. Computationally, the induced-subgraph Gibbs sampler is 2.2 times faster than the marginal-likelihood sampler, requiring 1.67 seconds versus 3.67 seconds per replication, consistent with a 2.6-fold speedup per likelihood evaluation.
212 At [n=500], the marginal-likelihood biases decrease to [-0.009,-0.022], whereas the induced-subgraph biases remain near [-0.080,-0.085], increasing the median relative discrepancies in [λ][1]and [λ][2] to 18.3% and 18.8%, respectively. The median relative discrepancy in the slope estimates also declines to 2.0%. The persistent difference in [λ] arises because re-row-standardizing the training subgraph changes the local scale of the network operators. In the simulated configurations considered here, the resulting adjustment in the estimated autoregressive parameters largely offsets this change in network scale, yielding negligible differences in holdout RMSE, 0.31% at [n=200] and 0.08% at [n=500]. Thus, the induced-subgraph estimator provides a computationally efficient surrogate when cross-block ties are unavailable, whereas the Schur marginal precision

259 Candidate Multiplex Relational Networks
260 Rather than imposing a single spatial weight matrix, the empirical analysis considers four candidate network layers representing different forms of firm-to-firm connectivity. 305 Sixty-Three-Month Panel
306 To assess whether the predictive improvement observed in August 2026 persists beyond a single cross-section, we evaluate the complete Multi-SEM pipeline over 63 consecutive months, from June 2021 through August 2026.
307 Dynamic Monthly Evaluation Design
308 At each rolling month, OLS residuals and Moran statistics are computed using only the [n][t][=80] training observations. Holdout outcomes [Y][*] remain unobserved during both network screening and parameter estimation. The covariates and correlation-based network layers are constructed exclusively from information dated before the target month. If no layer survives Benjamini–Hochberg control at 0.05, the archived program retains layers with [p<0.10]; if that set is also empty, it retains the layer with the smallest [p]-value. The selected layers are then re-estimated using the induced training subgraph, and the holdout returns are imputed from the full-graph precision matrix. A corresponding no-kriging specification uses the same coefficient estimates but predicts solely from the regression mean.
309 Across the 63 monthly cross-sections, the layers selected by the screening procedure vary substantially over time. The trailing-return correlation network or its resolvent is selected in 51 of 63 months (81.0%), the Yahoo Finance sector network in 35 months (55.6%), and the Yahoo Finance industry network in 33 months (52.4%). Thus, the network structures associated with residual dependence are not constant over the evaluation period. Table 13 summarizes predictive performance across all 63 monthly cross-sections.

348 9. Conclusion
349 Multi-SEM provides a framework for cross-sectional regression when disturbances propagate through multiple network layers. By modeling network dependence in the disturbance process, the framework preserves [E][Y][X][=Xβ] under exogeneity.
350 Four components underpin the reported analysis. First, the restriction [‖λ][‖][1][<1] provides a uniform sufficient condition for spectral invertibility over the parameter space and avoids the non-invertibility problems associated with restrictions based only on the signed sum of the network coefficients. Second, residual Moran's [I] is evaluated using its exact design-conditional moments; when a candidate layer satisfies [κ>3] or [ψ>0.20], its [p]-value is obtained from a [B=499] conditional Gaussian Monte Carlo procedure before Benjamini–Hochberg control is applied. Third, clique resolvents that reproduce a base layer, as identified by an off-diagonal correlation of at least 0.99, are removed before screening. Fourth, holdout imputation exploits the precision representation by solving a system involving the [n][*][×][n][*] holdout block rather than factorizing the [n][t][×][n][t] training covariance.
351 The simulation results show that the oracle specification reduces mean holdout RMSE by [8.6%] relative to OLS, while the screened specification achieves a [6.1%] reduction. At [n=200], the 95% credible intervals for the two active network coefficients exhibit finite-sample undercoverage, with coverage rates of [87.5%] and [89.5%]. The induced-subgraph estimator provides a computationally efficient surrogate when cross-block ties are unavailable, whereas the Schur precision [P][T] defines the coherent training likelihood when the full network topology is observed. In the Boston tract application, screened Multi-SEM with precision kriging reduces mean holdout RMSE by [28.5%] across 50 splits, whereas the specification that does not condition on contemporaneous peer residuals performs worse than OLS. Across 63 monthly S&P100 cross-sections, Multi-SEM outperforms OLS in 44 months, with a two-sided sign-test [p]-value of 0.0022. Here, too, the predictive improvement is primarily attributable to the contemporaneous kriging correction rather than to changes in the regression mean. The Newey–West test for the monthly loss differential yields [p=0.072], indicating weaker evidence once serial dependence in the monthly losses is taken into account. Extensions to dynamic panel settings and non-Gaussian outcomes remain topics for future research.
