S4.5 Longitudinal 63-Month Holdout Performance

===== 298 =====
Table 13 reports aggregate holdout performance over 63 consecutive months from June 2021 through August 2026. Figure 5 plots the month-by-month holdout RMSE, while Supplementary Figure S8 compares the mean RMSE across the competing model specifications. Across the full evaluation period, the mean holdout RMSE is {RMS}{E}{Multi}{=0.06568} for Multi-SEM and {RMS}{E}{OLS}{=0.06845} for OLS.

===== 303 =====
Across the 63 months, {d}{=0.000403,} and the conventional paired test gives {t}{62}{=3.60, p=0.00063.} The mean monthly RMSE difference, {RMS}{E}{OLS}{,t}{-RMS}{E}{Multi}{,t}{,} is {0.00278,} with a 95% paired-{t} confidence interval of {0.00115}{ }{0.00441}{.} Because monthly loss differentials may be serially dependent, an autocorrelation-robust comparison is also reported. If the squared-error differentials are treated as uncorrelated, the Diebold-Mariano-type statistic is {3.60.} Using a Newey-West long-run variance estimator with lag 3, the statistic decreases to {1.80,} with a two-sided normal {p}-value of {0.072} (Diebold & Mariano, 1995; Newey & West, 1987). Thus, the sign test and conventional paired test provide significant evidence that Multi-SEM improves monthly holdout performance, while the autocorrelation-robust comparison provides weaker evidence. Reporting these results together makes clear that the strength of the longitudinal comparison depends partly on how serial dependence in the monthly loss differentials is treated.
